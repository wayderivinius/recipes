# Clarified Spiced Rum & Apple Milk Punch

Crystal-clear, silky batch cocktail. Acid curdles whole milk; casein binds haze and tannins; you filter until the drink is transparent. Keeps for weeks in the fridge.

**Yield:** about 2–2¼ cups (4–6 servings) after filtering  
**Active time:** 15 minutes  
**Rest + filter:** 1–3 hours  
**ABV:** roughly 14–16% with 80-proof rum (wine-strength; a 3–4 oz pour is about one standard drink)

## Ingredients

### Base

- 1 cup (8 oz) aged rum — see Recommendations
- ½ cup fresh lemon juice (about 3–4 lemons)
- ½ cup rich demerara syrup (2:1 sugar:water), or 1:1 simple syrup
- ½ cup 100% apple juice or unfiltered cider
- ½ cup whole milk, **not** ultra-pasteurized

### Optional upgrades

- 2 oz strong black tea or lightly steeped chai, cooled — tannin and spice backbone
- ¼–½ oz allspice dram (St. Elizabeth or homemade)
- Pinch of kosher salt
- 2–3 wide lemon peels muddled with the sugar first (oleo saccharum) for brighter citrus oil

Demerara syrup gives more body than white 1:1. A little tea is classic in English-style milk punches and keeps the apple from tasting flat. Allspice dram echoes “spiced rum” without the vanilla-bomb of many commercial bottles. Salt sharpens everything.

## Recommendations

### Rum

- Everyday aged: Appleton Estate Signature or 8 Year Reserve, Plantation Original Dark / Barbados 5, Mount Gay Eclipse, El Dorado 5 or 8
- Explicitly spiced: Chairman’s Reserve Spiced (cleaner than Captain Morgan or Kraken)
- Splurge: Diplomático Reserva Exclusiva, or a split of Appleton + a splash of Smith & Cross

Avoid very cheap spiced rums; added sugar and artificial spice fight the clarification.

### Apple juice

Use 100% juice, preferably not from concentrate. Martinelli’s Gold Medal works. Fresh-pressed cider is better if you have it. Skip “apple cocktail” blends.

### Milk

Whole milk only. Look for “pasteurized,” not “ultra-pasteurized” (UHT curdles poorly). Organic grocery brands or local dairy are fine. Do not use skim, 2%, almond, or oat.

### Equipment

- Two pitchers or large measuring cups
- Fine-mesh strainer
- Paper coffee filters (basket filters work; large commercial ones are faster)
- Clean bottle for storage

## Method

1. **Make the punch base.** Combine rum, lemon juice, syrup, apple juice, and any upgrades (tea, allspice dram, salt). Stir until the syrup is fully dissolved. Taste and adjust sweetness now — it will taste a little sharp; clarification softens edges.

2. **Curdle.** Pour the ½ cup milk into a pitcher first. Slowly pour the acidic punch mixture *into* the milk (not the other way around). You will see immediate curdling. Give it one very gentle stir to distribute, then leave it alone.

3. **Rest.** 30–60 minutes at room temperature is enough for this batch size. You can also cover and refrigerate 4–24 hours; a longer rest often gives slightly clearer, silkier results.

4. **Filter.** Line a fine-mesh strainer with a paper coffee filter. Set it over a clean pitcher or jar. Pour the curdled mixture in gently. The first ½ cup or so will be cloudy — pour that back through the filter. The cake of curds that forms does most of the work. Let it drip; do not press or squeeze. Repeat through the same filter 1–2 more times until the liquid is brilliantly clear.

5. **Bottle and chill.** Transfer to a clean bottle. Refrigerate. Ready once cold. Keeps 3–4 weeks easily, sometimes longer.

## Serving

Pour 3–4 oz over a large ice cube in a rocks glass or Nick & Nora.

Garnish with a thin lemon twist, grated nutmeg, or a thin apple slice.

Optional lengthener: a splash of soda or sparkling cider.

The finished drink should be pale gold, completely transparent, with a velvety texture, rum and baked-apple notes, and a clean lemon finish. The milk solids are gone; what remains is body and softness.

## Strength

With 8 oz of 40% ABV rum in 24 oz of mixed ingredients, the batch is about **13.3% ABV** before filtering. Volume loss in the curds usually lands the bottled punch around **14–16%**. Adding 2 oz of tea drops that closer to **12–14%**. A 43% rum (Appleton 8 Year, some Plantation bottles) adds a point or so.

Comparisons:

- Stronger than almost any beer (typical lager/IPA 4–7%; big imperial stouts 8–12%)
- Same band as table wine (12–14.5%); weaker than port/sherry (17–20%)
- Lighter per ounce than a Daiquiri/sour (~18–25%) or Old Fashioned/Manhattan (~28–35%+)

Sugar, acid, and the milk-derived texture make it drink easier than the ABV suggests.

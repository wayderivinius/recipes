# Easy Stovetop Mac and Cheese

Weeknight stovetop mac from Joshua Weissman’s “Best Mac and Cheese (2 Ways).” A small roux, chicken bouillon, smoked paprika, and a mix of cheddar, American, and smoked gouda. No bake.

**Yield:** 3–4 servings  
**Prep:** 5 minutes  
**Active time:** about 20 minutes

Source: [Joshua Weissman, The Best Mac and Cheese (2 Ways)](https://www.joshuaweissman.com/recipes/the-best-mac-and-cheese-2-ways), also shown in [The Best Mac And Cheese At Home (2 Ways)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UhByCuLYt2g). This is the easy stovetop version, not the baked one.

## Ingredients

- Kosher salt, to taste
- 8 oz (227g) small elbow macaroni
- 2 Tbsp (28g) unsalted butter
- 2 Tbsp (19g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tsp (7g) chicken bouillon powder (Lee Kum Kee or Knorr)
- ½ tsp (2g) smoked paprika
- ½ tsp (2g) onion powder
- 1¼ cups (300mL) whole milk
- ½ cup (120mL) heavy cream
- 1 cup (90g) sharp cheddar, grated
- 1 cup (90g) American cheese, grated (block, or chopped Kraft singles)
- ½ cup (45g) smoked gouda, grated (Havarti works if that is what you have)

## Method

1. **Boil the pasta.** Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Salt it until it tastes like the sea. Add the macaroni and cook 1–2 minutes less than the package says, so it is just shy of al dente. Do not oil the water. Drain, reserving about 1 cup of the pasta water.

2. **Make the roux.** While the pasta cooks, melt the butter in a medium saucepan over medium heat. When it is bubbling, whisk in the flour. Cook, whisking, 1–2 minutes (the video cooks it 30–60 seconds) until the raw-flour smell is gone and the roux is pale gold.

3. **Season the roux.** Whisk in the chicken bouillon, smoked paprika, and onion powder.

4. **Build the sauce.** Slowly pour in the milk, then the cream, whisking the whole time so it does not lump. Bring to a gentle simmer and whisk until it coats the back of a spoon, about 2–3 minutes. It should still look a little loose; the cheese will thicken it.

5. **Melt the cheese.** Take the pan off the heat. Stir in half of the cheddar, American, and gouda until melted and silky, then whisk in the rest. Taste. The bouillon is already salty, so add salt only if it needs it.

6. **Finish.** Add the drained pasta and toss until every piece is coated and the sauce looks emulsified. If it is too thick, stir in reserved pasta water a tablespoon at a time. Serve right away.

## Notes

- American cheese is doing the smoothness work. Skipping it makes the sauce more likely to break or turn grainy.
- Grate from blocks. Pre-shredded cheese is coated and melts worse.
- Keep the sauce off a hard boil once the dairy is in.
- The baked version in the same video is a different recipe: 1 lb large elbows, cheddar and Gruyère, a bigger roux, and a 325°F bake with a broil at the end.
